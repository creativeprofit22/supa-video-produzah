# Project glossary

- **Clip gain** — The audio level adjustment stored in milli-decibels. Zero means unchanged level; mute is a separate track state.
- **AI account sign-in** — Connecting a Claude plan account (not an API key) through Anthropic's PKCE paste-code flow. Tokens live only in the OS keyring and never cross IPC; native code refreshes them and calls the Messages API with them. Starting sign-in requires an explicit terms-risk acknowledgement. See `docs/features/ai-account-sign-in.md`.
- **Audio fades** — Linear-amplitude fade-in and fade-out durations measured in sequence frames of the clip's output timeline, after speed conversion. Their combined duration cannot exceed the clip duration; zero fades mean no envelope.
- **Narrative beat** — One ordered unit of a first-cut plan: a span of script or transcript text with a planned start/end in microseconds, a language, optional must-show / must-not-show terms and an orientation preference. Beats come from a script (fixed words-per-minute) or from transcript segments with real word times.
- **Shot intent** — The typed kind of visual a narrative beat wants: owned footage, A-roll, title, lower-third, map, chart, screenshot or still-image motion. Only owned footage and A-roll resolve to media clips; the others resolve to a fallback graphic.
- **First cut** — A reviewable, content-hashed proposal that assigns a ranked media candidate or an explicit unresolved fallback to every narrative beat. Nothing reaches the timeline until the user applies it; applying commits one command group onto new tracks and undoes in one step. See `docs/features/first-cut.md`.
- **Fallback graphic** — The deterministic template (title, lower-third or unresolved note) a beat gets instead of a media clip. Title and lower-third shot intents become a caption card on the "First cut titles" track, with no marker. Unresolved beats — footage with no rights-safe match, map/chart/screenshot/still-motion intents, or beats a reviewer leaves unresolved — become an unresolved-note caption plus an orange "Unresolved" marker.
- **QC finding** — One time-ranged result of a quality check on a render, with a kind (black frames, freeze, silence, clipping, loudness off target, subtitle out of bounds, reading time short, text outside safe area, text overlap, missing media, repeated asset, uncovered beat, must-show missing, must-not-show present, motion stutter, motion drift, motion cut jump, cut off music beat, music fit, shot length out of range, steady shot run), a severity and a source (deterministic, editorial or rights). Its `findingId` is a SHA-256 of kind, source, subject, decisecond-rounded range and revision state hash, so it excludes frame size and is stable across delivery presets.
- **Severity** — `blocker` (stops delivery until resolved), `warning` (shown, never blocks) or `info`. A blocker is resolved only by an accept-anyway override; rights blockers can never be overridden.
- **Editorial evaluation** — The revision-level editorial findings (repetition, beat coverage, must-show / must-not-show, motion smoothness, pacing) computed from the exact revision being rendered, bound to its state hash and sent with every render request. A missing or mismatched evaluation fails the render before encoding.
- **Render manifest** — The immutable `<output>.manifest.json` written beside an export before it is promoted: revision and plan hashes, toolchain, input and output digests, loudness, QC status and findings. It never contains review decisions and is never rewritten.
- **Review record** — The append-only `<output>.review.jsonl` beside an export holding review decisions (accept anyway, repair attempt, repair stopped), each bound to the output and manifest digests.
- **Accept anyway (override)** — A recorded review decision, with a reason, that resolves one non-rights finding for release.
- **Repair attempt** — One proposed fix for a finding, routed through the normal proposal/approval path and recorded in the review record; at most three per finding before repair stops.
- **Release policy** — The rule that turns manifest findings plus review decisions into `releasable` or `blocked` with the unresolved finding ids.
- **Graphics description** — The versioned JSON input of the graphics renderer: canvas size, frame rate, duration in frames, one font file and up to 64 rect/text layers whose x, y and opacity are keyframed by frame index. It is validated strictly; anything unknown or out of bounds is rejected. See `docs/adr/0002-graphics-render-engine.md`.
- **Graphics overlay clip** — The transparent QuickTime Animation (`qtrle`, ARGB) `.mov` rendered from a graphics description. The export path composites it like any other video clip on a track above the footage.
- **Graphics track** — A sequence track of kind `graphics` that holds graphics clips. It has no audio, can be hidden and locked, and is shown read-only on the timeline until a graphics editor exists. See `docs/adr/0003-graphics-clips.md`.
- **Graphics clip** — A versioned (`graphicsVersion: 1`) project entity on a graphics track: a timeline start and duration, a caption font key and up to 64 graphics layers. Export turns it into a graphics description and composites the rendered graphics overlay clip over its time range.
- **Graphics layer** — One rect, text or image (still-image asset) inside a graphics clip, with keyframe tracks for x, y, scale, rotation and opacity.
- **Keyframe track** — An ordered list of keyframes `{ timeMicroseconds, value, easing }` for one layer property. Times are relative to the clip start; before the first keyframe the first value holds, after the last the last value holds.
- **Easing** — The curve that shapes the segment from a keyframe to the next one: linear, a named preset, cubic-bezier, spring (bounce, duration) or steps. The math is ported from animejs and is identical in TypeScript and Rust.
- **Motion preset** — A named function (slam, pop, tiltIn, cursorDrag, rockSlide, staggered entrance, text reveal) that fits keyframe tracks to a clip's duration. Applying one is a single undoable command.
- **Motion smoothness** — Editorial warnings scored from each graphics layer's x/y keyframe tracks, sampled at the sequence frame rate while the clip is on screen (scale, rotation, opacity and text-unit offsets are not scored). A move is a run of frames above the rest speed (0.25 px/s), split at every cut — a start or end frame of a clip on a visible video track, strictly inside the picture. `motion_stutter`: a move of at least 0.2 s with SPARC < −1.8 or LDLJ < −11.5. `motion_drift`: a move whose peak speed is under 25 px/s, or a stretch under 25 px/s lasting 0.5 s or more. Spring settle wobble directly after a fast move is not drift: a slow move starting under 0.5 s after a move peaking at 25 px/s or more (or after another such settle move), with no cut in between, skips the peak-speed check, though its slow stretches still count — a departure from diffusion-studio-2. `motion_cut_jump`: the layer is moving on both sides of a cut. Ported from diffusion-studio-2, tuned at 25 fps; durations and px/s speeds are converted to frames at the sequence rate, and the score thresholds hold unchanged at 24–60 fps. Hidden graphics tracks are skipped. Subject is `<graphicsClipId>:<layerIndex>`.
- **Text reveal** — A motion preset that splits a text layer into words or letters (its units) and fades and lifts each unit in turn.
- **Agent graphics description** — The compact, strictly validated JSON an agent or rule writes to ask for on-screen graphics: `schemaVersion: 1`, an optional style recipe id, and up to 64 `card` (title, lower third, end card) or `caption` items with text, start time in microseconds, optional duration and optional preset, reveal, font or colour overrides. It never contains layers or keyframes. Not to be confused with a graphics description (the renderer input). See `docs/adr/0005-agent-graphics-proposals.md`.
- **Graphics proposal** — A proposal (`proposalKind: "graphics"`) that offers graphics clips compiled from an agent graphics description, one reviewable item per clip. It may only create one empty graphics track and add graphics clips to it; nothing changes until the user approves, and a partial approval may apply only offered items with byte-identical commands.
- **Style recipe** — A named, frozen look for agent graphics: font, palette, entrance preset per card role, caption reveal and pacing (card duration, minimum gap, most graphics per minute, title and end card on or off, music beat snapping). Built-ins: punchy short-form, calm explainer (the default) and retro. See `docs/features/agent-graphics.md`.
- **Delivery preset** — A named output shape for Deliver: frame size (16:9, 9:16 or 1:1), H.264/AAC MP4, caption mode (burn-in or SRT/VTT sidecar), thumbnail frame and safe area. Deliver renders one job per preset from one reviewed revision.
- **Safe area** — The inset of the frame, per side in permille, that text must stay inside. Each delivery preset defines one (16:9 and 1:1: 5 % each side; 9:16: top 12 %, right 12 %, bottom 20 %, left 6 %, clear of social-app UI); other frames use the preset with the same aspect ratio, else 5 %.
- **Auto-fit** — At export, an unrotated graphics text layer whose laid-out text would cross the safe area at its resting position is first shrunk (down to 80 % of its size, never below 12 px), then wrapped at that size. Measurement uses real font shaping (parley) with the clip's font file.
- **Reading time** — The time on screen a caption cue or graphics text needs: its characters (excluding line breaks) at 17 characters per second. Shorter is a `reading_time_short` warning.
- **Music beat** — One detected pulse time in a music audio asset, in source-time microseconds. A downbeat is the first music beat of a bar. Music beats are audio analysis results and are never narrative beats: code says `musicBeat` / `MusicBeat`, never a bare `beat`, and never reuses narrative beat types. On the timeline, music beats come only from clips on unmuted audio tracks whose audio role is music.
- **Music beat analysis** — The derived-media artifact (`music_beats`, format `music-beats-v1`) of one music asset: detector (`beat_this` — the Beat This! final0 model run as ONNX by the bundled `supa-beat-detect` sidecar on CUDA or the CPU, with models and the optional GPU pack in the managed music-beat runtime folder (ADR 0004) — or the in-app `tempo_fallback`), tempo in BPM, sorted music beat, downbeat and onset times. It is produced by a cancellable music beat detection job and keyed by the asset's content identity plus detector id and version. See `docs/features/music-beats.md`.
- **Pacing finding** — An `info` editorial finding about shot rhythm. Shots are the intervals between cuts. `shot_length_out_of_range`: a shot shorter than 1 s or longer than 7 s. `steady_shot_run`: four or more consecutive shots of equal length (within ±1 frame). With music beats present, `cut_off_music_beat`: a cut further than the tolerance (1 frame, at least 40 ms) from its nearest music beat; and one `music_fit` summary with the on-beat cut fraction and the shot-length to music-beat-period ratio. Ported from diffusion-studio-2 `checker/music-fit.ts` and `checker/rules/timing-rules.ts`.

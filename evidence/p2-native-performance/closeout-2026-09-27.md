## 2–4. Visible-layer drop attribution (harness only; no app change)

Each event carries the decoder counters at that instant plus `clipId`, `active`, computed `visibility`/`display`/`opacity`, `isConnected`, `onlyVideo` and the derived `visible`. `frame` events also carry `quality` and `attribution`; `decoder-segment` and endpoint records carry the last attribution. ## 5–6. Final-seek latency: cause and outcome

- Diagnosis: `final-seek-diagnosis.md` (script `final-seek-diagnosis.mjs`, output `runs/final-seek-diagnosis-dKm4Tu/result.json`, sha256 `cc93cfcaf61c558121ee45227dba04366713e4259b9805c427175b8e3c15e46f`).
- **Cause recorded:** the export's keyframe interval (GOP length). Final seek latency tracks the distance to the previous keyframe with Pearson r 0.986–0.991 across all four Final cells. The export has a uniform 250-frame (8.3 s) keyframe interval, libx264's default, because the export argv sets no `-g`. Seeks under 1 s from a keyframe reach p95 36–41 ms; seeks 4 s or more away reach p95 302–309 ms. The Preview control, on source media with the same keyframe interval, shows the same linear law at about half the per-second cost. This is a codec-level property of the delivered file, not of the preview architecture.
- **Secondary, unexplained:** the Final per-second decode cost is about 2× the Preview cost (37 vs 18.5 ms/s). Codec parameters don't explain it, and neither does in-flight network loading (the same slope holds on idle-network seeks). It is recorded as unexplained.
- **Decision on the export GOP:** the user was asked whether to shorten it (the ask was dismissed without an answer). Per the plan's default, the miss is **accepted and documented**: exported files are unchanged and no app code was edited. The 250 ms target came from the measurement plan, not from the phase criteria. Shortening the export GOP remains available as a separate, user-approved change; a model estimate (not a measurement) puts the p95 near 83 ms with a 2 s interval, at the cost of larger exports.
